## Paper 1

- **Title:** Drug Resistance Prediction Using Deep Learning Techniques on HIV-1 Sequence Data
- **Authors:** Margaret C. Steiner, Keylie M. Gibson and Keith A. Crandall
- **Year:** 2020
- **Journal:** Viruses, 12(5), 560
- **DOI or URL:** 10.3390/v12050560

### Research Objective
What question was the study investigating?
The study aimed to compare three deep learning architectures for predicting HIV-1 drug resistance and investigate whether important model features corresponded to known drug resistance mutations.

### Methods
What biological data, computational tools, or statistical methods were used?
The researchers used publicly available HIV-1 genotype and phenotype data from Stanford University's HIV Drug Resistance Database, covering 18 antiretroviral drugs.

They compared three deep learning models:
- Multilayer Perceptron (MLP)
- Bidirectional Recurrent Neural Network (BRNN)
- Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)

They used 5-fold stratified cross-validation, permutation-based feature importance and phylogenetic analysis.

### Key Findings
What were the main results?
- CNN models generally performed best among the three architectures.
- Important model features often corresponded to known HIV drug resistance mutation positions.
- Model interpretability helped researchers understand which genetic features influenced predictions.
- The study demonstrated the potential of deep learning for HIV-1 drug resistance prediction.

### What I Learned
What new scientific or technical knowledge did I gain?
### What I Learned

- HIV-1 drug resistance can be predicted using viral genetic sequence data and machine learning.
- Different machine learning architectures can be compared to identify models with better predictive performance.
- Cross-validation is important for evaluating model reliability and reducing the risk of overfitting.
- Model interpretability helps identify genetic mutations that contribute to drug resistance.
- **Machine learning predictions should be compared with previously established scientific findings and known HIV drug-resistance mutations to assess their biological relevance and consistency with existing knowledge.**
- Predictive performance alone is not enough; biological interpretation and validation are also important in computational biology research.

### Relevance to My Research
How does this paper relate to bioinformatics or my HIV-1 drug-resistance research?
This paper is directly relevant to my HIV-1 drug resistance machine learning project.

It provides a scientific foundation for using viral genetic mutations to predict antiretroviral drug resistance. It also motivates comparing machine learning models and applying interpretability techniques to understand biologically important mutations.
